Applications of analyses

The potential for application of my analysis and modelling differs between each generated analysis.

The referee based profiling showed the most promising and novel data: Penalty eagerness, wholesale referee percentiles, referee impact on matches, and the foul maps.

These could all be used by clubs, if not as primary datapoints as secondary datapoints that can inform players what defending styles to adopt for a certain referee, and identify potential weak points in an opposition teams style of play (i.e. zones where they get caught on the counter a lot and commit fouls, or get isolated and commit fouls).
With further statistical investigation, bookmakers may potentially pay for this data too if you can provide evidence of mispriced lines.
Administrative bodies such as the PGMOL in the UK may pay for data that profiles their referees in such a way, or less established bodies with smaller operating budgets and limited ability for in house data science.
Anti corruption bodies could use this data as an initial flag into suspicious referee behaviour.
Lastly, the media and online sports apps could use these figures to provide deeper punditry and analysis.

The player form prediction could also be used by clubs as an adjunct tool to manage rotation within squads to try and maximise player performance peaks. This prediction data could also be used by sports apps and fantasy apps to provide lineup predictions.

Lastly, the market based modelling was largely disappointing, and at this current stage probably couldn’t be commercialised or utilised, but with successful iteration could be useful to almost all players listed above.

One caveat to this was the referee cards predictor, which I generated after finding statistically significant referee deviance in awarding of cards. Although the hold out predictions were a small sample size, with the correct thresholds it achieved reliable prediction.
